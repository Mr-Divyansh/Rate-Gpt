How to read the records

A guide to minimum, maximum and modal mandi prices

The table is a view of reported market observations, not a live offer or a guaranteed sale price. Check each record's market, crop, arrival date and unit before drawing a comparison.

Minimum price

The lowest price recorded for the crop in the selected market observation. It describes the lower end of that record's range; it is not a promise that every seller received this amount.

Maximum price

The highest price recorded for the crop in that observation. Quality, grade, lot size and trading conditions can differ, so the maximum should not be assumed for all produce.

Modal price

The most frequently reported price in the market record. It is a useful reference point, but it is not necessarily the price available for a particular farmer's crop.

How to compare two markets

Compare the same crop and, where possible, similar arrival dates and units. Check whether the market names and districts match the locations you intend to compare. Include transport, loading, commission, quality deductions and time costs before deciding where to sell.

Why a record may be missing

A crop or market may not appear when the source has not published a matching observation, when filters return no match, or when the upstream service is temporarily unavailable. A missing result does not mean that no trading took place.

Source, timing and limitations

Rate-GPT retrieves government-reported mandi observations through the Government of India's Open Government Data platform and related AGMARKNET records. The source controls publication schedules, corrections and completeness. A fetch timestamp indicates when Rate-GPT requested data; it is not the date the market transaction occurred. Always use the arrival/record date shown with each result and confirm important decisions with the mandi or relevant official source.
